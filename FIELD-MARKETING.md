# Field Marketing

## Events Should Create More Than Booth Traffic

I don't believe an event is successful because people visited the booth.

An event should have a business objective.

That could be:

* Meetings
* Opportunities
* Pipeline
* Executive relationships
* Partner engagement
* Market awareness
* Customer expansion

But the objective should be defined before the event begins.

---

# The Event Lifecycle

```text
STRATEGY
   ↓
TARGET ACCOUNTS
   ↓
PRE-EVENT
   ↓
ON-SITE
   ↓
MEETINGS
   ↓
POST-EVENT
   ↓
PIPELINE
```

---

# Pre-Event

The event begins before the conference.

I build:

### Target Account List

Who do we want to meet?

### Outreach

How do we create meetings?

### Executive Engagement

Who should leadership meet?

### Content

What will create a reason to engage?

### Experience

Why should someone choose to spend time with us?

---

# On-Site

The booth is only one component.

Potential activations:

* Executive meetings
* Customer meetings
* Dinners
* Private events
* Partner meetings
* Product demonstrations
* Analyst meetings
* Content capture

The goal is to create valuable interactions.

---

# Post-Event

The event isn't over when the booth closes.

Follow-up should include:

* Meeting recap
* Lead/account routing
* Sales follow-up
* Personalized content
* Opportunity creation
* Executive follow-up
* Nurture

---

# Event ROI

I want to understand:

### Investment

* Booth
* Sponsorship
* Travel
* Staff
* Creative
* Events
* Dinners
* Swag
* Advertising

### Output

* Meetings
* Opportunities
* Pipeline
* Revenue
* Customer engagement
* Partner engagement

---

# Field Marketing Principle

Don't ask:

> "How many people came to our booth?"

Ask:

> **"What business did this event create?"**
