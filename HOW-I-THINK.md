# How I Think About Marketing

## Marketing Is a Revenue System

I don't think about marketing as a collection of disconnected activities.

Content, paid media, events, ABM, product marketing, PR, email, social, and sales enablement are all pieces of the same system.

The job of marketing is to connect those pieces into a machine that creates awareness, earns attention, generates demand, creates pipeline, and ultimately contributes to revenue.

```text
MARKET
   ↓
ICP
   ↓
POSITIONING
   ↓
MESSAGING
   ↓
CAMPAIGNS
   ↓
ENGAGEMENT
   ↓
MEETINGS
   ↓
OPPORTUNITIES
   ↓
PIPELINE
   ↓
REVENUE
   ↓
EXPANSION
```

If something isn't working, I want to understand where the system is breaking.

---

# 01 — Start With the Buyer

The biggest marketing mistake is starting with the product.

I start with the buyer.

Before I write messaging or launch a campaign, I want to understand:

* What problem are they experiencing?
* Why does the problem matter?
* What happens if they do nothing?
* How are they solving it today?
* What alternatives are available?
* Who owns the problem?
* Who influences the decision?
* What triggers a buying process?
* What objections exist?
* What language does the buyer actually use?

The goal isn't simply to understand the customer.

The goal is to understand **why they buy.**

---

# 02 — Define the ICP

An ICP isn't simply:

> Companies with 1,000+ employees.

That's a demographic filter.

A useful ICP tells us which companies are most likely to have:

1. The problem
2. The urgency
3. The budget
4. The authority
5. The ability to implement
6. The reason to change

I look at four categories.

### Firmographic

* Industry
* Company size
* Revenue
* Geography
* Growth
* Organizational structure

### Technographic

* Existing technology
* Security stack
* Infrastructure
* Cloud environment
* Identity architecture
* Endpoint architecture

### Behavioral

* Buying signals
* Content consumption
* Intent
* Website activity
* Event attendance
* Product research

### Trigger Events

* Security incidents
* M&A
* Cloud migration
* New executive leadership
* Compliance requirements
* Technology refresh
* Workforce changes
* Existing vendor dissatisfaction

The best accounts aren't simply companies that **could** buy.

They're companies that have a **reason to buy now.**

---

# 03 — Position Before You Promote

Marketing cannot compensate for unclear positioning.

If a company sounds exactly like five competitors, buying becomes difficult.

I ask:

### What category are we in?

### What problem are we solving?

### What alternatives exist?

### Why is our approach different?

### Why does that difference matter?

### What proof supports the claim?

The goal is to create a simple mental model that a buyer can remember.

---

# 04 — Make Complex Things Simple

Cybersecurity is complicated.

Buyers don't have time to understand every technical detail before understanding why something matters.

That doesn't mean marketing should dumb down technology.

It means marketing should **translate technology into business value.**

For example:

**Technical capability**

→

**Operational impact**

→

**Business outcome**

→

**Why the buyer should care**

The technical details still matter.

But they should support the story rather than become the story.

---

# 05 — Build for the Entire Funnel

I don't believe marketing stops when someone books a meeting.

The marketing system should support the entire buyer journey.

```text
Awareness
   ↓
Education
   ↓
Interest
   ↓
Evaluation
   ↓
Validation
   ↓
Purchase
   ↓
Expansion
```

Each stage needs different content, messaging, experiences, and measurement.

---

# 06 — Marketing and Sales Are One Team

Marketing shouldn't throw leads over the wall.

Sales shouldn't operate without marketing intelligence.

The strongest relationship looks like:

```text
MARKETING
     ↕
SHARED DATA
     ↕
SALES
```

Marketing should provide sales with:

* Account intelligence
* Buying signals
* Messaging
* Competitive information
* Content
* Campaign context
* Persona insights
* Objection handling

Sales should provide marketing with:

* Objections
* Lost deal reasons
* Customer language
* Competitive intelligence
* Buying patterns
* Product feedback

The feedback loop makes both teams better.

---

# 07 — Strategy Must Become Execution

A 60-page marketing strategy that never gets implemented is worthless.

I prefer:

**Clear strategy → focused priorities → fast execution → measurement → iteration.**

I want to know:

* What are we doing?
* Why are we doing it?
* Who owns it?
* When does it launch?
* What does success look like?
* What happens if it doesn't work?

---

# 08 — Measure What Matters

I care about activity.

But I care more about outcomes.

A simplified model:

```text
Marketing Activity
        ↓
Engagement
        ↓
Qualified Meetings
        ↓
Opportunities
        ↓
Pipeline
        ↓
Revenue
```

The further down the funnel we can connect marketing activity to revenue, the better.

That doesn't mean awareness metrics are useless.

It means they shouldn't be the final answer.

---

# 09 — Kill What Isn't Working

Marketing teams often accumulate programs.

Another campaign.

Another event.

Another newsletter.

Another social channel.

Another tool.

Another report.

Eventually, activity becomes the strategy.

I believe marketers should regularly ask:

> If we stopped doing this tomorrow, would the business notice?

If the answer is no, it deserves scrutiny.

---

# 10 — Build Systems, Not Heroics

I don't want marketing to depend on one person remembering everything.

I want:

* Repeatable processes
* Clear ownership
* Documented playbooks
* Automated reporting
* Defined KPIs
* Consistent campaign processes
* Sales alignment
* A reliable operating cadence

The goal is to build a marketing organization that gets stronger over time.

---

# My Core Belief

Great marketing sits at the intersection of:

**Customer understanding**

*

**Clear positioning**

*

**Creative execution**

*

**Operational discipline**

*

**Revenue accountability**

That's where I do my best work.
