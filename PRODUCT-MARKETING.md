# Product Marketing

## Turning Technology Into a Reason to Buy

Product marketing sits between product, marketing, sales, and the customer.

I think of the role as the translation layer.

```text
PRODUCT
   ↓
TECHNOLOGY
   ↓
VALUE
   ↓
BUYER PROBLEM
   ↓
BUSINESS OUTCOME
   ↓
MARKET MESSAGE
```

---

# Positioning

Before promoting a product, I want to know:

* What category are we creating?
* What problem are we solving?
* Who has the problem?
* What alternatives exist?
* Why is our solution different?
* Why does the difference matter?
* What proof do we have?

---

# Messaging Architecture

I typically structure messaging as:

```text
Company Narrative
       ↓
Value Proposition
       ↓
Messaging Pillars
       ↓
Proof Points
       ↓
Personas
       ↓
Use Cases
       ↓
Features
```

Notice where features appear.

Near the bottom.

Features matter.

But buyers generally don't purchase features.

They purchase outcomes.

---

# Buyer Personas

For every important persona, I want to know:

### Their Goals

What are they measured on?

### Their Problems

What prevents them from achieving those goals?

### Their Priorities

What matters most right now?

### Their Objections

Why might they reject the product?

### Their Buying Criteria

What must be true before they purchase?

### Their Language

How do they describe the problem themselves?

---

# Competitive Intelligence

Competitive analysis shouldn't be a feature checklist.

I want to understand:

* What competitors claim
* What buyers believe
* Where competitors are strong
* Where competitors are weak
* What category language they own
* What objections they create
* Where there is whitespace

The output should help sales win.

---

# Launch Strategy

A launch should have three layers.

## Internal

Sales and customer-facing teams understand:

* What changed
* Why it matters
* Who it is for
* How to position it
* How to demo it
* How to handle objections

## Market

The market understands:

* What changed
* Why it matters
* Why now
* Why this company

## Demand

There is a reason for qualified buyers to engage.

---

# Sales Enablement

Product marketing should create assets sales actually use.

Examples:

* Sales deck
* One-pager
* Battlecard
* Competitive guide
* Discovery questions
* Objection handling
* Demo narrative
* Persona guide
* Email templates

The goal isn't to produce documents.

The goal is to improve sales conversations.

---

# Product Marketing Test

Before launching anything, I ask:

> Can a salesperson explain this in 30 seconds?

If not, the messaging probably isn't finished.

Then:

> Can a buyer understand why they should care?

If not, the positioning probably isn't finished.

Then:

> Can we prove the claim?

If not, the story needs stronger evidence.
